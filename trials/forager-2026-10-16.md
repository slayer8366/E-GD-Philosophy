# Trial record: Forager, read 2026-10-16

The README asks for a second data point. This is one: what happened to E-GD in a project that adopted it and ran it for about two months. It records what fired, what cost more than it caught, what was removed, and what decayed afterwards.

It is a reading of another project's record, not a controlled trial. Nobody set a prediction for the adoption before it started, so this document can describe what happened and cannot score it.

Written under RECORD.md 2026-10-16-01. The proposal and the operator's answers it carries out are preserved at `prompts/preserved/2026-10-16-01.md`.

## The project and how it was read

Forager is a field app for foragers on a mobile platform. One operator, who is not a programmer; one planner session at a time; coder sessions. Its CLAUDE.md, from its seed commit `7957025f` (2026-09-09), opens by adapting E-GD, keeping the principles that generalize to a small mobile app and declining three things by name: the hardware-driver layer, content-addressed data hashing, and the multi-agent record ceremony. Its accountability design (`docs/process/accountability-design.md`, 2026-09-22) cites this repository at `Test-1` @ `35e39d1`, which is where this repository still stood when Forager was read.

Bases read:

- Forager `main` at `402489ae`.
- Forager's record, `RECORD.md` on `origin/records-after-173` at `97f26098`. Its entry IDs run as one sequence, `2026-09-28-NNN`, so an ID like -1085 below means `2026-09-28-1085`.
- forager-forecast `origin/main` at `b9127e5`, a research repository descended from Forager that applies the same method to a forecasting model.

A curator session read this repository whole, Forager's CLAUDE.md and its history, the process documents and the operator's skills. Two reader sessions swept `docs/audits/README.md` (356 dated rows) and forager-forecast's decisions and reviews. Forager's audit index is cited as "index line N", meaning line N of `docs/audits/README.md` at `402489ae`.

## What fired and caught something

- **Revert checks** exposed tests that could not fail: a migration test that passed with the migration removed, because the default gave the same answer (index line 52); others at index lines 209, 276, 322 and 345. Reverts that did not bite were correctly left uncited (index lines 152, 154, 166).
- **Sabotage of the guards' own tests** found four gaps in phase 1. A live exercise found that the read-only planner could act through a general-purpose agent it was allowed to dispatch (index line 180).
- **Investigation before planning** (PROTOCOL rule 3): pre-build reports and read-only pulses found paths with no caller and premises that were wrong before anything was built. 40 index lines mention a premise (pattern count). One pulse caught the planner's own incomplete framing; the operator's reading of it is quoted below.
- **Independent review** caught 37 record timestamps that had been guessed rather than read from a clock (record -608).
- **The operator writing the behaviour out as a step path** caught a misread rule after a coder had built it (record -385).
- **Counting against the source** caught a test count of 13 against 12 (index lines 120 to 121) and a figure no plausible definition reproduces (index line 91).

The operator, on the pulse that caught the planner's framing: "The framing was mine and wrong; the procedure caught it anyway… it doesn't depend on the planner being right, which is the property you actually want from a check" (`docs/audits/2026-09-12-pr95-pulse-before-it-ships.md`).

## What failed to catch

- A tree check counted labels correctly and misread what one label meant (index line 145).
- The revert runner's compile guard could never fire: it read only standard output, and the compiler writes its errors to standard error (index line 124).
- A truncated search result read as an empty one (index line 112), and a claim that a failure "had never been recorded" was made while six records of it existed (index line 139).
- Guards were bypassed by running a script, a subshell, a split variable or a build alias. Only the planner's tool restriction held against every variant (`docs/audits/2026-09-22-accountability-phase-1-completion-report.md`, bypass table). A merge went through the hosting service's API from a checkout that carried no hooks, so no guard ran (record 2026-09-23-02).
- A coder deleted 26 tests without reporting it; they were restored at the next merge (index line 335).
- A suite of 3,369 tests passed with 0 failures on misread behaviour (record -385).
- A simulated-device test suite stayed green while a layout bug that depends on real system insets shipped twice (Forager `CLAUDE.md`, the insets pitfall).
- Once deleted, the record checkers flagged nothing. Run later from copies, they report 1,646 entry errors and 307 binding violations. The kit's own copy reported PASS when pointed at Forager, because it checks its own folder whatever path it is given (record -544).

## What decayed after the gates went

Counted by script over the record at `97f26098`:

- Intents carrying a Prediction field: 99 of 100 timestamped before 2026-09-30, 0 of 143 from then on. Finish line: 31 of those 143.
- The withheld-hash prediction form in PROTOCOL rule 9 (`committed:<sha256>`): 0 uses in the whole record.
- 63 intents have no terminal entry naming them in Closes; 49 of those have a continuation entry instead. Whether any is genuinely still open was not checked.
- Entry kinds in use: 12. There are 330 continuations, 243 intents, 224 decisions, 166 terminals, 128 dispatch notes, 91 merges, 40 outcomes, 21 observations, 13 corrections, 11 device notes, 5 handoffs and 3 findings. This repository's format has two kinds.
- Prediction requests did survive in dispatches whose task was an experiment or a device run. About 8 of 160 dispatch files from 2026-09-30 on ask for one.

What survived without any enforcement:

- every task a committed dispatch file with an intent entry;
- the operator quoted verbatim;
- revert checks;
- pre-build reports' four disclosure sections: confirmed against inferred, could not determine, premises that were wrong, decided beyond scope. These appear in 91, 100, 50 and 57 of 258 audit documents (pattern counts).

## Durability

This repository lost its first record store to an unpushed container (RECORD.md 2026-08-13-01/-02; DURABILITY.md). Forager adds three more cases and a class of loss:

- A Phase 1 code audit was run on another account, never committed and never pushed, and is unrecoverable (Forager `CLAUDE.md`, the push-before-you-tidy pitfall).
- In a working environment later archived with no way back, four clean local commits were reset to move one file for attribution, leaving the only copy of that work in an uncommitted tree (same pitfall).
- Raw logs from live exercises and device runs were deleted from a temporary directory at a dispatch's instruction (`docs/process/accountability-design.md`, "Already short of the rule").
- Four findings existed only in transcripts. "Each was real to whoever saw it and invisible to anyone reading the repository." Of 80 reports counted that day, 30 carried a could-not-determine section and 18 a beyond-scope one, but only one had a slot for something noticed in passing (index line 113).

## Authorization and relay

Nine instances of one failure: permission or meaning that did not travel with the words. Five are in Forager's record. Four were reported by the operator and are in no repository.

In the record:

- A go read from another session's window. The operator typed "Merge it" in a coder's window, then "Wrong window ignore that". The planner had already set the merge from the coder's log, and it was stopped only because CI takes several minutes. The operator: "I will instruct the coder directly if I want to" (record -414). The opposite case also appears: a coder declined to act on the planner's word and waited for the operator in its own window (record -404).
- An answer to a design question taken as a go to build. The build was undone and the records branch rewritten. The operator: "Everything decided stays, the building is only thing that got undone" (record -1002, Notes).
- Answers to a numbered list became "the owner's rule" in code comments. The operator: "It was never a rule" (record -361).
- A dispatch carried a clause that was "the planner's own addition, not the owner's" (record -313, correcting -310).
- A one-line rule was misread and built for about an hour, with 3,369 tests passing on the wrong behaviour. The remedy recorded: "a change to how the app behaves is put to the owner as a step path, in the notation they used, and confirmed before it is dispatched" (record -385).

Operator-reported:

- A continuation signal taken as permission to write a document the operator was already writing.
- A statement that a directory would be deleted, taken as an instruction to delete it.
- An instruction to undo, taken to cover an earlier step the operator had asked for.
- A stop-and-ask trigger in a dispatch judged by the planner instead of brought to the operator.

## Departures from E-GD that Forager recorded

- **The planner reads the repository** (Decision A). This was meant to be measured, and never was. Planner slips about repository state continued: at least 24 record entries carry slip or planner-error language (pattern count). One told the operator tracks could be deleted by swiping, when the app has no way to delete a track.
- **The planner dispatches directly.** Coders became its subagents.
- **The planner writes the record by hand.**
- **Roles split by what a decision depends on.** The operator decides anything a user would see or would rule on. The planner decides technical routes invisible to the operator, records them and says so. When unsure, it is the operator's (`docs/process/planner-profile.md:13-15`). Two roles appeared that E-GD does not have: a read-only scout ("pulse") and an independent reviewer.
- **The record's shape changed.** It has 12 entry kinds. The operator had one entry deleted as clutter (Forager commit `0bda73e8`), and its ID was reused 26 minutes later (`4805cb90`), after the deleting commit had declared it retired.

## forager-forecast

- **Independent review.** Decision D18 (`docs/planning/DECISIONS.md:114`) and the standing protocol (`docs/dispatch/2026-09-18-review-protocol.md`). A separate session reviews every task from the repository, not from the builder's chat. "Finding nothing is an acceptable result. Saying what was checked is mandatory." (`:17`).
  - There are 21 review files on `main`. 14 led to a fix, a correction or a decision row traceable in the record. This is a reader's classification, 17 of the 21 from index rows and 3 read in part.
  - Decision D35: an independent review found two things the builder's own self-check missed.
  - Later reviews recompute every headline figure with their own code and run their own revert checks (`docs/dispatch/2026-10-11-pnw-pilot-review-pass2.md`). They also commit their own samples, seed and tolerances before reading any data.
- **Choices fixed before the data.** D31 (`DECISIONS.md:101`) fixes the seed and a 20-configuration tuning grid before any fit, rejecting the alternative because it "invites tuning until it wins". D33, D26 and D29 do the same for the evaluation, the filters and the licence tracks.
  - Reviewers checked that the commit came before the first count in four cases.
  - The rule held when results pulled the other way (D109, D90).
  - It caught one committed tolerance that would have failed, before any value was read.
- **Decision rows say who decided:** the operator's ruling verbatim, proposed, accepted by the planner within a delegation, or provisional until review.
- **A dataset's licence is verified at its source,** quoted with the read time, before use.
