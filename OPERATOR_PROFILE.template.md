# Operator profile

What [the operator] wants, and how they want it, for [project]. Every role reads this at the start of each session, before its first action. It is started at onboarding (ONBOARDING.md) and grows as preferences come up.

The project's standing instructions govern. Where this profile disagrees with them, this profile is wrong.

## How this profile is kept

- **Every line is the operator's own words,** quoted, with the date and where they were said: a record entry, a preserved prompt, or the session. A line nobody can source does not belong here.
- **A line goes in only when the operator says yes.** When they state a preference, or correct how they were asked or told something, the role proposes a line quoting them and asks. Clarify first, record once (RECORDING.md, "What goes in").
- **A one-time override is never written in.** The operator using their authority once is not a new preference (OVERVIEW.md, "You are in charge"; PROTOCOL rule 22).
- **A change supersedes.** The line is replaced, and the record entry that changes it quotes the old one.
- **Answers stay answers.** The operator's words are quoted, never renamed as rules.

Each line looks like this:

- **[The preference, in a few words].** "[the operator's words]" ([date]; [where]).

## Who the operator is

What bears on how things are explained: their background, what they read easily and what they don't, the devices they answer from.

## How to ask them

The form of a question, how many at a time, whether a recommendation comes first, how options are shown, what to do when an answer is unclear.

## How to report to them

Length and order, plain words or technical, tables, how bad news and slips are told, the time zone for times.

## What they decide

Merges, the go to build, approvals, what they delegate and to whom. When unsure, it's the operator's.

## Standing instructions

Writing rules, naming, what stays private, anything always or never done.
